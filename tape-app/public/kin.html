Call a person. Not a room.

Type a name and their Mac rings. No link to paste, no account to make, no waiting room to sit in. Answer someone once and calling them back is one tap.

Green means they can hear you.

A soft light lives at the edge of the window. Green while you speak, blue while you listen, and it rises and falls with your voice — so you always know you are getting through, without looking away from their face.

One voice at a time.

Kin gives the floor to whoever is speaking, the way a room does. Nobody talks over anybody, and nobody hears themselves come back.

The voice is the recording. The picture is the camera's own.

Every other call compresses you through a design from 2011 that spends your face when the network dips. Kin never does. Your voice arrives as it left you. Your picture is the one your camera saw.

Under 150 milliseconds. Anywhere on Earth.

Between two people there is exactly one delay that is real: the time light needs to cross the distance. Everything else is a defect, and we are removing it. Free on Mac today, measured on live calls with every release, and not stopping until the only thing between two people is the light.

Open it

On a Mac: open the .dmg, double-click Kin. macOS warns once, because we sign it ourselves and Apple has not notarised it: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway (or right-click the app → Open). That is the only step — after it, the app moves itself into /Applications and keeps itself up to date. Nothing else to do.

On that warning: click Done — not "Move to Trash" — and then Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Dragging the app to /Applications still works if you prefer, but it is no longer needed: opening it once is enough, and every update after that is signed and applied by the app itself.

curl -fsSL https://room.tokkah.com/macos/install.sh | sh — a download by curl is never quarantined, so it never meets Gatekeeper.

On Android: open the downloaded .apk. Android asks once, because this comes from us rather than from Play: tap Settings and allow your browser to install apps. That is the only step. Kin keeps itself up to date after that, the same way it does on a Mac.
