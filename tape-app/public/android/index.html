Kin for Android

The same call as Kin for macOS, on a phone. Not a port of a web page and not a second product: it speaks the same wire, byte for byte, so a phone and a Mac are in the same call with nothing translating between them.

Install

Download kin-0.128.0-android.19.apk

Android asks once, because this comes from us rather than from Play: open the downloaded file, and when it says it cannot install from this source, tap Settings and allow your browser to install apps. That is the whole of it. There is nothing to fill in afterwards — the app opens with your camera on and takes a name for this phone by itself, so somebody can call it.

To be reachable with Kin closed, open the … menu and turn on Let people reach you when Kin is closed. Android shows a quiet notification the whole time it is listening; that is not a choice we get to make, and it is the honest price of a phone that can be rung. It holds one HTTPS request open and nothing else — no camera, no microphone, no socket.

Use it

Type the same handle on both ends, or paste a link somebody sent you. Two phones, or a phone and a Mac, find each other through a rendezvous at room.tokkah.com and then talk directly, peer to peer. The room name is also the encryption salt, so choose something only the two of you would say — and say it to each other rather than typing it anywhere.

The code on screen during a call is the safety code. Read it aloud: if it matches on both ends, nobody is in the middle.

The mouth-to-ear number, honestly

10.4 ms is what the Mac measured for audio arriving from the phone. The other direction — what your ear experiences on the phone — is now measured by the phone itself and reported in the call's record, with the device buffer as its basis because Android does not expose what its audio path adds after that. Android's path is slower than CoreAudio's; the number says by how much rather than hiding it.
