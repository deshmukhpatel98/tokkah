The build is illustrating a script instead of directing attention. Its most important subject—a person—is its least convincing drawing. Its most distinctive product behavior—the edge light—is treated as decoration. And it repeatedly makes the audience read an explanation of something the image has not made them feel.

The replacement should have one governing idea:

**The film begins by losing someone. Kin brings them back—and then stops getting in the way.**

Keep the restraint. Remove the smallness, the diagrammatic storytelling, and the constant ambient reassurance. Make a 72-second film with a recognizable person, an interrupted visual phrase, and a light that finally learns to stay at the edge.

All coordinates below are on the 1920×1080 stage. Numeric production instructions are not additional on-screen copy.

## 1. Diagnosis: the ten biggest problems

1. **S1–S2: the protagonists are screensavers.**  
   Two soft circles cannot carry conversational embarrassment, anticipation, or affection. Nine hundred pixels of separation communicates layout, not distance. The film needs two presences with different rhythms—not two objects assigned different colors.

2. **S2: the collision is the wrong emotional metaphor.**  
   Shattering particles turn a familiar, awkward interruption into a miniature science-fiction battle. Talking over someone feels like a failed handoff: you start, hesitate, start again. Show that rhythm. Do not explode it.

3. **S3: nothing valuable exists before it deteriorates.**  
   A square becoming more square is not a picture being lost. The audience must recognize a person before the image fails. Likewise, a random stepped line cannot communicate a damaged voice because there was never an identifiable phrase to damage.

4. **S4: a clean cut is not a release.**  
   A snap only has force when the viewer can compare the same thing immediately before and after it. Changing the whole shot throws away that comparison. Preserve the composition, the person, and the interrupted gesture. Repair them in place.

5. **S5–S7: the app is being presented like a portfolio mockup.**  
   Brown discs behind a floating card say “generic glass interface.” The card has no optical relationship to the room. Typing takes too much dramatic space because nothing meaningful changes when the name becomes a person.

6. **S8: the portrait fails the film’s central promise.**  
   A head-shaped blur is not someone. Recognition comes from the relationship between skull, neck, shoulder, hair, clothing, and light—not facial features. The current build is trying to substitute blur for drawing.

7. **S8–S9: the product’s signature behaves like an alarm.**  
   A thick, saturated perimeter across the title bar dominates the person. The light should reassure you peripherally. Instead, it demands inspection. The cramped side-copy then makes the frame feel like a product slide.

8. **S9–S10: every escalation makes the subject smaller.**  
   Pulling back to demonstrate both sides is useful once. Shrinking again abandons the emotional subject precisely when the film should be establishing trust. The enormous empty center is not confidence; nothing is happening there.

9. **S11–S13: the argument becomes less credible as it becomes more ambitious.**  
   The faint globe is difficult to read, the moving counter collides with geography, and the worldwide arcs resemble a stock network animation. Then a long historical accusation appears over two dots. The film stops showing a working product and starts making declarations.

10. **S14–S15, score, poster: the conclusion has not been earned.**  
    Overlapping gradients, a large wordmark, an italic promise, and a major chord could finish almost any technology film. The dimmed poster is worse: it hides the product and advertises a video player. The ending should resolve something we have already experienced. The poster should make someone want to meet the person inside the window.

## 3. Shared direction and implementation rules

1. **Use an optical drawing system, not more glow.**

   Ground remains `#05060a`. Build room and skin values only from the supplied palette and mixtures with the ground. Remove amber.

   Light has three components:
   - A readable form.
   - A narrow luminous contour.
   - A restrained atmospheric spill.

   Never use the spill as a substitute for the form.

2. **Use three canvases total, including the cache canvas.**

   - One visible `1920×1080` scene canvas for portraits, light, degradation, and globe.
   - One hidden `4096×2048` atlas canvas for procedurally generated portraits, softened room fields, and glow sprites.
   - One `256×144` grain canvas, scaled to the stage.

   Draw UI, copy, borders, and controls with DOM.

   Generate portrait softness once at initialization. A deterministic separable blur over the relevant atlas regions avoids relying on animated CSS blur or browser-specific canvas filter behavior. Do not create temporary canvases.

   Seed grain from `floor(t × 60)` in every playback mode, not only export. Use neutral grain at effective opacity `0.025`; disable it for reduced motion.

3. **Separate image motion from text motion.**

   Entrances use `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)`. Continuous breathing uses a sine. Copy fades in over `500ms` with `12px` rise and fades out over `400ms`. The cue times below mean “start appearing”; exit times mean “start fading.”

   At rest, round text translation to whole stage pixels. Never scale a text-bearing UI during a held reading beat.

   Story copy is `64px`, except the opening at `88px` and S4 at `72px`. Side-copy is `56px`. Only one story cue is visible at a time.

4. **Keep the edge behavior, but fix its optical construction.**

   Preserve:
   - `48–96px` depth.
   - The `(1-u)^2.2` inward ramp.
   - `1.5px` perimeter hairline.
   - Brightness `0.55 + 0.45 × envelope`.
   - `90ms` attack, `260ms` release.
   - Thickness smoothing of `450ms` attack and `1200ms` release.
   - `400ms` color handovers.

   Clip it to the call viewport below the title strip. The title strip never glows.

   Do not add four full-strength gradient layers at the corners. Compute the glow from the minimum distance to the viewport boundary, so corners do not receive double or quadruple brightness.

   Keep the entire head envelope at least `128px` inside the source viewport. The glow has finite support and ends at `96px`; there is therefore always at least `32px` of clean separation from the face.

   Precompute the smoothed envelopes at `1000Hz` from authored syllable events. `render(t)` samples them; it never updates a running envelope from the previous frame.

5. **Replace the anonymous score with a recognizable exchange.**

   Remove the continuous drone, typing ticks, connection chime, travel sweep, and big reveal chord.

   Build two related, wordless sound materials from seeded noise exciting damped resonators:
   - “You”: resonances at `174`, `348`, and `870Hz`.
   - “Them”: `233`, `466`, and `1165Hz`.
   - Partial levels: `0`, `−12`, and `−20dB`.
   - Pan only `0.10` left and right.
   - Phrase peaks around `−26dBFS`; call-edge versions around `−34dBFS`.
   - No intelligible speech and no simulated mouth synchronization.

   Use the same three-event rhythm as the light. The first act interrupts it; the call lets it finish.

   Add a quiet harmonic bed only after connection: `146.83`, `220`, and `293.66Hz`, combined around `−34dBFS`, with a `1700ms` entrance. At the reveal, add the upper octave over `1200ms`, not a struck chord.

   Render the complete score to an audio buffer for live playback as well as export. Starting playback from offset `t` then preserves every tail and silence when seeking.
